# Report

Q1. Four class pairs account for about a third (32%) of the 18,109 wrong training labels: truck and automobile (13.1% of errors, counting both directions), cat and dog (9.7%), deer and horse (5.1%) and airplane and ship (4.1%). Deer labeled as dog is also common (2.7%). All of these are mostly visually similar classes, so the noise is concentrated on specific confusions and not spread evenly across classes.

Q2. Memorization of wrong labels appears to begin around epoch 25 and accelerates after about epoch 35. Until roughly epoch 20, accuracy on the mislabeled training subset, measured against the noisy label, stays near 0.13 to 0.15, so the network mostly predicts the true class there. It then rises to about 0.30 by epoch 40 and 0.85 by epoch 60. Over the same period, clean-label validation accuracy falls from a peak near 0.79 (epoch 22) to 0.65, and noisy-label validation accuracy plateaus and then declines.

Q3. Each image was scored by its mean training loss over the 60-epoch noisy-label run. This gave an AUROC of 0.954 and precision of 0.86 on the 18,109 top-scored images (random: 0.5 and 0.40). Cross-validated confidence (0.924) and loss at the early-stopped epoch (0.931) performed slightly worse. The score appears to miss mislabeled images whose wrong label is a plausible class, mainly truck labeled automobile, deer labeled horse and cat labeled dog, probably because the network fits these labels consistently and their loss stays low.

Q4. Co-teaching gave the highest noisy validation accuracy (0.573), above filtering (0.570), GCE (0.550) and cross-entropy (0.528 early-stopped, 0.478 final). Unlike the baseline, its accuracy did not decline late in training, which suggests resistance to memorization. A plausible reason is that each network learns only from samples the other finds low-loss, and wrongly labeled images tend to have high loss. Part of the gain may come from averaging two networks, and the forget rate used the measured noise rate. The gap to filtering is within validation noise. One seed only.

Q5. Within the two methods compared, the ranking did not change: co-teaching beat plain cross-entropy under both noise types, with a similar gain in clean validation accuracy (about 8 points on human noise, 7 on synthetic). Absolute accuracy was higher on synthetic noise (0.88 vs 0.84 for co-teaching), probably because random errors give no consistent wrong class for the network to learn, whereas human errors concentrate on similar-looking pairs. Only two methods were run on synthetic labels, so a change in the order of the other methods cannot be ruled out. Results are from a single seed.
